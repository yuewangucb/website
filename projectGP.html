Physics-Informed World Modeling: Regression Powered Anomaly Detection and Forecasting


Building autonomous systems and robust world models requires more than static predictions—it demands the ability to capture dynamic physical reality on the fly and act before failures cascade.


To tackle this in an extreme operating environment, I developed a closed-loop perception and control pipeline leveraging particle accelerator multi-sensor logs from SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory.


Key highlights of the pipeline:


  • Physics-Informed World Dynamics Model: Built a non-parametric world model from scratch utilizing Gaussian Processes equipped with custom Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernels. The architecture trained online directly on continuous, live sensor data streams, capturing non-linear system dynamics in real time.
  • Low-Latency Anomaly Flagging: Ran online GP regression over continuous data streams to compute real-time fitted values, predictive uncertainty bands, and derivatives. Evaluating deviations across these metrics triggered low-latency alarms within just two time-series steps, enabling instantaneous trajectory alterations and rapid human-in-the-loop decision-making.
  • Stochastic Simulation & Edge-Case Synthesis: Parsed massive streams of raw physical sensor logs to architect a stochastic simulator powered by linear dynamics and Gaussian noise. This simulated sandbox synthesized critical edge-case anomaly distributions and robust control datasets that were otherwise too risky or rare to capture in live operation.
