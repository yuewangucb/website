Mining Signal From 20+ TB of Raw Images

Modern perception and multi-modal models are only as good as the raw sensor data fed into them. When physical sensors generate massive, degraded data streams, isolating high-fidelity signal from ambient noise becomes the central engineering bottleneck.


I built high-throughput computer vision and signal processing pipelines to tackle over 20 TB of corrupted ultrafast laser diffraction imagery, resulting in a co-authored publication in Science.


Key highlights of the pipeline:


  • Spatiotemporal Background Subtraction: Formulated a data-driven background subtraction model to decouple a static spatial noise pattern whose amplitude scaled nonlinearly from frame to frame, isolating faint dynamic targets without suppressing signals.
  • Spatial Transformation at Scale: Implemented high-performance data-cleaning algorithms to re-orient, de-noise, and correct spatial distortions across tens of terabytes of multi-dimensional arrays, reconstructing accurate reciprocal-space coordinates.
  • High-Impact Downstream Discovery: Restoring high-fidelity spatial signals unlocked a core physical discovery: tracking the transient evolution of charge density waves on picosecond scales under laser-quenched conditions.

Whether you are mining subtle patterns across multi-modal sensor streams or training vision-language models on terabyte-scale messy spatial data, robust preprocessing and domain-adapted representation pipelines make or break downstream intelligence.
